Melbourne Playgrounds

A redesign of the Melbourne Playgrounds website

Role
Sole UX researcher & UX/UI designer
Responsibilities
Heuristic evaluation, product redesign, prototyping, usability testing, data analysis
Timeline
12 weeks

Project intro

Melbourne Playgrounds is a public resource for families to find playgrounds and activities across Melbourne and Victoria. Despite it being a valuable platform, the site suffered severe usability problems that made basic tasks slow and hard to complete.

This redesign project will involve heuristic evaluation of the original site, moderated and unmoderated usability tests, a UX/UI redesign in Figma, and A/B comparison testing to measure improvements.

The design process

  1. 01 Heuristic evaluationApply UX principles to identify issues
  2. 02 Usability testingDesign and conduct moderated and unmoderated tests
  3. 03 Analyse & ideateAnalyse data and redesign by issue severity
  4. 04 RedesignFigma mockups and prototyping
  5. 05 A/B comparisonRepeat same tests on redesigns, analyse and compare results

01 Identifying the problems

5/5Difficulty

Standards & Conventions

The images on both pages give the impression that they are clickable or interactive, but they are not. In contrast, the search bar appears non-interactive, as it resembles an image, despite being an active input field. This violates user expectations of interaction behaviour.

3.5/5Difficulty

Visual Hierarchy

The articles section dominates the homepage, concealing the website’s primary purpose of finding playgrounds and activities.

4/5Difficulty

Instructions Must Die

Instructions were displayed throughout the entire website, and was required for many tasks. Features should be intuitive and easy to follow, minimising the need for instructions.

4/5Difficulty

Hick’s Law

Throughout the website, users were required to select regions (e.g. South East, Central, etc.) to access relevant lists or maps. For users who are unfamiliar with which suburbs belong to each region, this process may be confusing and require significant trial and error before finding the desired information.

02 The tests

A combination of moderated and unmoderated usability tests with both qualitative and quantitative measures was used to validate known usability issues and gain deeper insights into user behaviours, challenges, and opportunities for improvement.

Think Aloud

3 participants

Participants spoke of their thought process aloud whilst completing 3 tasks on the site. Each task was timed. This revealed where they hesitated, what confused them, and how they recovered from errors.

SEQ Test

3 participants

After each think aloud task, users rated its difficulty on a 7-point rating scale: 1 is very difficult, 7 is very easy. This captures perceived difficulty immediately after each task.

Initial Impressions

3 participants

Users were shown the “Water Fun” page and asked what they expected certain elements to do and where to find particular information. This tested standards and conventions and visual hierarchy.

SUS Score

3 participants

An industry standard usability scale completed after all tasks. The higher the score, the better the usability. Captures the users’ overall perception of usability and acts as a benchmark.

First Click Test

10 participants

Users saw the homepage and were asked where they would click to find a playground near them. Time and accuracy were recorded. First click showcases task success and efficiency. This tests visual hierarchy.

5 Second Test

10 participants

The homepage was shown for 5 seconds. Users then had to answer what the site was about, what stood out to them, and where the search bar was. This tested whether the page communicates its purpose and features quickly enough.

03 What was revealed

5 Second Test

9/10 couldn’t identify the search bar

Only one participant confidently knew where the search bar was. It’s unconventional styling meant users didn’t recognise it as an interactive input.

5 Second Test

4/10 couldn’t identify the site’s purpose

These participants gave incorrect answers. This indicated that there was an issue with visual hierarchy.

Think Aloud & SEQ

The map task forced trial and error from everyone

The task involving the map feature scored longest time on task and worst SEQ score: 2/7. One participant didn’t complete it.

Think Aloud & Walkthrough

All 3 users expected image cards to be interactive

Participants believed that the image cards should have been interactive and contain more information when clicked on.

Think Aloud & SEQ

Fast Find was hard to discover without prompting

Participants struggled to find the Fast Find feature, despite it being the most functional search feature. It lacked visual prominence.

SUS Score

28.3 average SUS Score

The industry average score is a 68. This affirms that the usability of this website is very poor.

What worked

Task completion was drastically faster and more accurate across all 3 tasks. The accuracy of the first click test went from 8/10 to 10/10. SUS scores improved from 28.3 to 85.8. This highlights that the redesign successfully addressed the UX issues identified in the original site.

Key finding

Results were not ordered by rating. Not all pages were redesigned or added into the prototype making some tasks take longer.

Future design response

Results should be ordered by rating. Test with higher-fidelity prototype.

Reflections

A/B testing and comparison confirmed that the redesign massively improved usability across all tasks and metrics. The SUS Score increased by 203%, and task completion times were drastically faster.

01

Testing on a Figma prototype rather than a website could have impacted accuracy of results. Some tasks were faster because fewer pages existed, others may have taken longer because certain pages or interactions weren’t built. Future testing could use more pages and higher fidelity prototypes.

02

The Water Fun page used collapsible alphabetical lists while the Playgrounds page used direct filtering. This is a design inconsistency between similar pages. A more united list pattern should be applied across all page categories.

03

The redesign increased visual prominence for the four distinct search methods (maps, Fast Find, search bar, and page level filters), each one serves a different purpose. This wasn’t always clear to users. There could be clearer visual differentiation through icons or labelling, or a common search method should be prioritised.

Rooted Faith

Bridging faith, environmental science, and community into one platform

Role
Sole UX researcher & UX/UI designer
Responsibilities
Primary and secondary research, product design, prototyping, user testing
Timeline
12 weeks

The problems

  1. 01Many Christian environmentalists struggle to find environmental information that is both scientifically credible and plainly aligned with their faith. This forces them to interpret and merge these perspectives independently
  2. 02Christian environmentalists lack trusted spaces to engage in environmental discussions that has a balance of credible information, respectful communication, and faith-based content
  3. 03Many churches don't actively integrate environmental stewardship into discussions, sermons, or community practices. This leaves Christian environmentalists feeling unsupported in connecting their faith to environmentalism

Project intro

Rooted Faith is a moderated discussion and learning platform for Christian environmentalists that incorporates scientifically credible information, a Christian lens, and respectful engagement from the community.

This platform includes a discussion forum for environmental issues, educational resources, and church and community involvement while distinguishing verified experts and researchers from general users to encourage credibility.

01 Understanding the community

Before any primary research was done, empathy mapping and context mapping were used to create assumptions and identify possible gaps and limitations in understanding. Secondary research showcased denominational trends when it came to views on human participation in climate change. The Anglican, Uniting, and Catholics churches had higher levels of belief that humans caused climate change.

02 The interviews

To understand both the target audience and those with opposing views, interviews were conducted with Christian environmentalists as well as a Christian participant who did not identify as an environmentalist. This provided insight into potential barriers, and opportunities for broader application.

Trust & credibility

Participants prioritised credible, scientific resources. They were skeptical of content derived from opinions and wanted verified expertise.

The faith lens

All four environmentalists saw creation care as an obligation shaped by faith. Christianity provided the ethical framework.

Quiet advocacy

Most participants preferred personal advocacy. This looked like lifestyle changes, productive conversations, and modelling behaviour.

The absence of church

Church engagement with environmental stewardship was inconsistent among the participants. Only one said it was core to their church’s identity.

02 Competitor analysis

There lacked a direct competitor as the target audience was highly niche. The closest alternative was Reddit, but the platform lacked constructive conversations, often hosting a hostile environment. Additionally, there were no spaces tailored to Christian environmentalists.

03 User persona

After analysing the responses of the participants, an average profile was derived from the findings. The user persona was then developed to represent the majority of participants, who demonstrated environmentally conscious behaviours rather than engaging in public advocacy

04 Concept development

Rapid ideation was used to brainstorm different concepts before converging into a single platform. A concept map confirmed that Rooted Faith would combine multiple separate user behaviours: researching, interpreting, and discussing, into one platform.

04 The core & pillars

Moderation was not treated as a single feature, but a core value of the platform, shaping every design and community decision.

01

Moderated discussions

This platform encourages users to participate in respectful environmental discussions.

02

Learn

Users can access educational content through article categories like: The Foundations & Deep Dive.

03

Church & community

Churches communities and groups can access Bible study guides and other church-related resources. Users can also discover and engage with churches and communities interested in environmental stewardship.

04

Verification & fact-checking

Verified researchers and experts are visually distinguished from general users. AI and verified members can also help identify misleading environmental claims.

Feature spotlight: verification system

The core response to the trust and credibility problem, allowing users to distinguish expert voices and facts from general opinions.

Gold badge: verified experts

Credentialed researchers and experts will have their real name and qualifications displayed instead of a username.

Green badge: general user

General users have usernames displayed. Though their contributions are valued, they are clearly differentiated from verified experts.

Fact check requests

Users can flag any post or comments for fact checking via the options menu. Requests are anonymous to reduce social risk and encourage honesty.

AI assistance

AI supports the fact-checking process as real people fact checking every post is not possible.

05 Concept validation

An initial impressions test was conducted on a past interviewee. The response indicated that there was a clear understanding of Rooted Faith’s purpose and target audience from the homepage.

What worked

Each page’s function was understood without explanation. Participant felt comfortable navigating, contributing to discussions, and using features.

Key finding

The platform did not feel welcoming to users with different viewpoints, this signals a possible echo chamber risk. Verification system caused confusion initially, but once explained, it was received positively and seen as a feature that builds trust

Future design response

Onboarding or context should explain verified vs. general users without requiring explanation. Future iterations to explore inclusiveness while preserving the community.

Reflections

User testing confirmed that Rooted Faith’s purpose can be immediately understood. The needs of the target audience such as: credible science, faith-based lens, and church implementation were directly addressed in the solution. Unfortunately, the platform is biased towards users who already hold environmentalists views, causing a potential echo chamber.

01

Designing for a niche community can create belonging, but risks singularity. Future iterations will have to explore ways to include and encourage other viewpoints.

02

The platform depends on verified expert participation. Real world implementation will require funding, partnerships, organisational support, or volunteering.

03

Niche community platforms will mean smaller user base. For a larger and more diverse user base, the platform would have to host discussion topics on several different issues that impact the Christian community as a whole.
